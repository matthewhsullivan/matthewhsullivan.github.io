Programs and ticketing at UConn’s von der Mehden Recital Hall

Marketing Assistant

Print design · Brand standards · Pricing · AI-built tools

The situation

Every concert needs a printed program, and almost all tickets were being bought at the door. About 90% of sales happened there, and management wants that closer to 45–50%.

What I did

  • Produced 5+ branded concert programs, turning editors’ manuscripts into print booklets that follow UConn’s brand standards.
  • Pitched advance online pricing and cashless messaging to move buyers toward purchasing ahead of time.

A tool I built: vdM Program Builder

Formatting each program by hand took me about an hour: copying the hall’s master Word template, pasting in the repertoire, program notes, and bios, then fixing the spacing and the page order for booklet printing. In October 2026 I started building an app to handle that step.

How I built it

I built it with Claude, an AI assistant, which wrote the code. I didn’t program it myself. My part was deciding what the tool needed to do, giving it the hall’s real templates and style rules, testing it on actual programs, and turning each round of reviewer corrections into new rules for it to follow.

What it does

  • Reads the program files that come in and fills a copy of the hall’s own Word template, panel by panel, in booklet print order.
  • Applies 90+ formatting rules from the hall’s style guide and flags anything that needs a person to decide.
  • Exports print programs (an editable Word file and a PDF for review) and digital programs, and lets me adjust text by hand before exporting.

What came of it

1 hour → seconds
of formatting per program
6+
programs produced with it since October 2026

Every program still goes through the hall’s editorial review before it’s printed. The app speeds up formatting, not proofreading.
